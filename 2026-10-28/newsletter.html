Annie can now query your PostgreSQL databases
Featured
Annie reads your PostgreSQL databases, read-only, during an investigation and ties what she finds to your infrastructure graph. She discovers schemas, tables and indexes, runs SELECT queries, explains query plans and ranks the slowest queries from pg_stat_statements. She also runs health checks across indexes, connections, vacuum, sequences, replication, buffer cache and constraints.
You pick the access scope: health and stats (the default), a diagnostics schema limited to the views you approve, or full read of application tables, opt-in only. Statements run in read-only transactions with a statement timeout, and passwords are encrypted at rest with AWS KMS. The integration is in beta and available on request at contact@anyshift.io.

Annie answers follow-ups in a Slack thread without a tag
Once Annie has joined a thread, she replies to follow-up questions and requests without an @mention. She reads the recent conversation and decides whether a message is for her, so teammate-to-teammate chat, short acknowledgments and ambiguous messages get no reply. You still need a tag for bot messages, threads she has not joined, and threads where someone pressed Stop on her latest answer.
It is on by default. The "Reply in threads without being tagged" switch lives on the Annie Automation default behavior card.

How to find dependencies before migrating Kubernetes workloads
Moving a workload to another cluster breaks what nobody wrote down: in-cluster DNS names, IAM trust policies tied to the old cluster, firewall and partner allow-lists tied to old IPs. The walkthrough builds one dependency inventory from four sources: declared Kubernetes objects, a week or more of real network traffic, what the workload's role can reach in your cloud, and hard-coded endpoints in code and DNS. Then it shifts traffic gradually and keeps the old deployment alive through a full business cycle.
